Brief

Cycle counting without a scanner is a trust problem, not just a data-entry problem

Businesses running ERP systems like Infor VISUAL depend on accurate on-hand inventory data, but not every warehouse has barcode scanners for cycle counts. Without one, counts get written on paper or typed into a spreadsheet, then reconciled against the system by hand, line by line. That process is slow, and it has a quieter problem underneath it: when the person counting can see the system's expected quantity while they count, it's easy, even unintentionally, to write down what the system says instead of what's actually on the shelf, which defeats the purpose of the count.

As the BA who understood exactly where this process broke down, I scoped the fix and used Claude as an AI build partner to ship it, closing both gaps: making the mechanical side of reconciliation fast and error-resistant, and make the count itself trustworthy.

What it needed to do

  • Accept a real Infor VISUAL on-hand export (CSV/XLSX) with flexible column mapping, not a fixed template
  • Let a counter key in physical counts as fast as a spreadsheet, including pasting a column straight from Excel
  • Prevent counts from being copied off the system instead of counted for real
  • Turn the comparison into a discrepancy report a supervisor can act on immediately, not a raw data dump
How It Works

Three steps: load, count, reconcile

01

Load the VISUAL export

Upload the on-hand inventory export as CSV or XLSX and map the part-number and on-hand-quantity columns, or load demo data to try the tool without a real file.

02

Enter physical counts

Key counts straight down a spreadsheet-style grid (Enter/↓ to advance, ↑ to go back), paste a column copied from Excel, or upload a separate counts file. Parts not counted are simply left blank.

03

Generate the discrepancy report

The tool compares every counted part to VISUAL, calculates variance and count accuracy, flags anomalies, and writes a plain-language summary with a recommended next action.

Key Features

Built-in controls, not just a comparison

Data integrity control

Blind count mode

VISUAL's on-hand quantities are hidden from the counter during entry, so a count can't be quietly copied from the system instead of physically verified. This is the one control I'd call the design decision the whole tool is really built around: a cycle count is only as good as the counter's incentive to actually count.

Root cause analysis

Pattern detection

When discrepancies cluster within the same part family, the tool surfaces that pattern directly ("check for a shared root cause: location, receiving process, or recent transactions") instead of leaving a supervisor to spot it manually across dozens of rows.

Quality check

Anomaly review flags

Rows carrying signs of a transposition, a unit-of-measure mix-up, or an unusually large variance are flagged for recount before any adjustment gets posted back to VISUAL, catching data-entry errors before they become inventory errors.

Reporting

Plain-language summary & export

Every run generates a short written summary of what was counted, what matched, what didn't, and what to do next, plus a CSV export and a print/PDF-ready discrepancy report for anyone who wasn't in the room for the count.

Result

19 of 20 parts counted and compared against VISUAL in a single pass, largest variances identified immediately (GSK-NIT-050 at -22, CM-H2-150 at -9, SA-BRC-6 at +9), and a clear recommended action generated automatically: recount flagged rows, investigate the clustered family, then post approved adjustments, turning what would be a manual afternoon of line-by-line checking into a single structured pass.
